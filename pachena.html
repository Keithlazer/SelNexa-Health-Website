SELNEXA HEALTH PROJECT · PROPOSED PILOT

Testing the AI tools young people use for sexual and reproductive health

Pachena is a lab SelNexa Health is setting up to check SRH chatbots in the open: whether they give safe answers in the languages people actually use, what they do with the most private data a person has, and who answers when they get it wrong.

Partner on the pilot All projects

Why Pachena

Young people everywhere now ask AI chatbots about contraception, pregnancy, HIV and relationships, often because they would rather not ask anyone they know. In Zimbabwe, where Pachena starts, SRH chatbots are already in use, many of them on WhatsApp and some in local languages.

The rules around these tools are arriving, one country at a time. In Zimbabwe, POTRAZ, Zimbabwe's data protection authority, began compliance inspections of organisations that process personal data in September 2026, and the National AI Strategy 2026 to 2030 includes a pillar on governance and ethics. What is missing is independent testing: nobody checks these tools in local languages, looks at what happens to the data, or gives users a place to complain. That gap is not unique to Zimbabwe.

Accuracy alone is not enough. A 2026 study of SRH chatbot answers in Nepali found that 43.8% of the accurate answers still had usability or safety gaps. Pachena looks at the whole picture: the answer, the data, and what happens afterwards.

Built to travel: the method, personas and scorecard work in any country and any language. Zimbabwe is the first pilot. Later rounds can test the tools young people use elsewhere in Africa and beyond, with local testers, local languages and each country's own guidance and data laws.

Where it stands: Pachena is a proposal. No tool has been tested yet, and the method will be published before the first round.

What Pachena does

Five parts: two test a tool, one reports on it, and two help people act on what was found.

Simulated client testing

Trained young people and nurses chat with each tool as fictional personas, such as a rural teenager, a young mother living with HIV or a person with a disability. Every question is asked in the languages the tool's users speak, starting with English, Shona and Ndebele in the Zimbabwe pilot, and scored against national and WHO guidance for accuracy, safety, referral, stigma and language.

Data and privacy checks

What each tool collects, where it is stored, whether it leaves the user's country and how long it is kept. Testers use their own data rights to ask for access and deletion, and record what happens.

Public scorecards

A two page plain-language scorecard for every tool, in the languages of the people using it, online and printed for health institution waiting areas. Developers see it first and their reply is published alongside it.

Pachena Line

A complaints channel for anyone harmed by an SRH AI tool, over SMS, USSD, WhatsApp, voice and paper. It runs on SelNexa's offline first technology, so reports made without a signal still arrive. Every case goes to a named party and response times are published.

Procurement kit

A readiness check, a procurement checklist and an accountability template for ministries, NGOs, donors and health institutions buying SRH tools, so data export, encryption keys, audit access and exit plans are written into contracts.

How Pachena stays independent

  • The method, persona scripts and scoring rules are published before the first test.
  • Paid pre-launch checks for developers never change a public score.
  • A community board of young people, nurses and civil society signs off every scorecard.
  • SelNexa Health is building its own SRH tool, ContraWise. An outside partner tests and scores it, and its results are published like any other tool's.
  • Every developer gets 14 days to reply before a scorecard is published.

Testers use fictional personas and never share real personal data.

The pilot: Zimbabwe first

A nine month pilot in Zimbabwe, in four phases. Each phase starts only when the one before it has met its gate.

Months 1 to 2: set up

Publish the method, confirm ethics requirements, recruit and train young people and nurses as testers, and form the community board.

Months 3 to 5: first test round

Test up to five SRH AI tools in three languages, run the data checks and send each developer a private report.

Months 6 to 7: publish

Release the scorecards, open the Pachena Line and share the procurement kit.

Months 8 to 9: policy

Brief the Ministry of Health and Child Care, the Zimbabwe National Family Planning Council and POTRAZ, publish the pilot report and plan the next round, including the next country.
