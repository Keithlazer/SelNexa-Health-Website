OUR MISSION

An infrastructure problem, not a knowledge problem

About 2.2 billion people are still offline, and 96% of them live in low and middle income countries (ITU, Facts and Figures 2025). Internet use is 85% in urban areas and 58% in rural ones. WHO projects a shortfall of 11.1 million health workers by 2030, mostly in low and lower middle income countries (WHO). In Zimbabwe, where we're based, there were about 23 doctors, nurses and midwives per 10,000 people in 2022 (WHO Regional Office for Africa), about half of WHO's indicative threshold of 44.5 (WHO, Workforce 2030). Global progress on universal health coverage has stalled: WHO's service coverage index rose only from 68 to 71 between 2015 and 2023 (News-Medical, on WHO's World Health Statistics 2026). The gap between the care people need and the care they can get is not a knowledge problem. It's an infrastructure, access, and systems problem.

We started SelNexa Health in Harare in 2024 because the software on offer to health institutions assumed stable power and fast internet. Ours assumes neither. We test in Zimbabwe first, and we design for any health institution where the power or the network can't be trusted.

Among other things, Africa CDC's New Public Health Order calls for stronger African public health institutions, more domestic resources for health, and respectful, action-oriented partnerships. We work in that frame. We're building a company that pays its way, because we don't think impact and revenue are in conflict.
